DEPOSITION

Case of Isaiah Brown , I. C., No. 598,425

On this 23 day of June, 1902, at
Beaufort, county of Beaufort,
State of South Carolina, before me, Read Hanna, a
special examiner of the Bureau of Pensions, personally appeared
Isaiah Brown, who, being by me first duly sworn to
answer truly all interrogatories propounded to him during this special
examination of aforesaid claim for pension, deposes and says: I live
about 12 miles n. w. of Beaufort S.C. on a
plantation called Old Teeheetee My
P.O. address is Beaufort S.C I get my
mail myself at the general delivery
window- my mail comes in the box
of Capt Christenson who always does
my writing, but I get it myself, Am
a farmer I do not know my age
but I count myself at 55 years I was
born on Old Teeheetee plantation where
I now live My owner was John Joiner
Smith (dead) His widow is dead and
he never had any children My father
was Cuffee, my mother, Diana, Brown
who both belonged to my owner and
are both dead I have one brother
named Harry Brown who lives on the
plantation where I reside I derive
my name from my father I have
never been known by any other than
my present name I dont know my
age but I was married two years
before the war and was the father of
one child when I enlisted I do not
know how old I was when I enlisted
I enlisted at the old Fort near
Beaufort Enlisted in February
I think but I do not know the
year I was discharged on Morris Island
