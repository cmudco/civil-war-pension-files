     Case of       Ben Williams                                No.  1424473.


            On this 12 day of   June         , 1924, at   Adams Run
     county of      Charleston         , State of South Carolina  before me,
       E. F. Fewell                , a Special Examiner of the Bureau of Pensions,
     personally appeared   Ben Williams               , who, being by me first
     duly sworn to answer truly all interrogatories propounded to him
     during this special examination of aforesaid claim for pension,
     deposes and says:
                                                                          6-5121

 1       I do not know my age.  Residence and mail address, Box 8,
 2   Adams Run, South Carolina.  I work some here helping in the potato
 3   crop.  That is a ll the work I do.
 4       My full complete name is Ben Williams and I am the same Ben Wil-
 5   liams who is applying for pension on account of service in Company
 6   B, 103 U. S. C. Infantry in the Civil war.  That is all the service
 7   I have ever had, I never served in the Navy, Coast Guard or Revenue
 8   Cutter service.  I did not serve in the World War and no member of
 9   my family served in that war.  I am not receiving nor have I applied
10   for compensation on account of the service of any person in said war.
11       I was born on a plantation about 5 miles from Savannah, Ga.  My
12   father was named Redd Williams and my mother was named Amelia.  We
13   belonged to Colonel James Legree and I was born on his place.  I
14   had one brother named Elijah but he is now dead.  He died at Savan-
15   nah some time ago.   He drew a pension on account of his service
16   in Company K, 103 U. S. C. Infantry.  We enlisted at the same time.
17       I enlisted at Hiltonhead, S. C.,I do not know the date of my en-
18   listment but I know it was in the Winter time.  I served in the
19   Army for about one year, I  now it was about time to plant cotton
20   when I was mustered out.
21       We did not stay long at Hiltonhead before we left there.  From
22   there we went to Port Royal but was only there a short time.  From
23   there we went to Honey Hill, S. C., We had a fight there that lasted
24   two days and a night.  That was the only fight we had.  We were at
25   Port Royal and Beaufort for a while after that and then we were
26   sent to Fort Pulaski and stayed there until we were mustered out.
