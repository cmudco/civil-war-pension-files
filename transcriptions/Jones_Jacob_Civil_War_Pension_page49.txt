DEPOSITION

Case of Susan Jones, No. 457,053

On this 26th day of December, 1896, at
Beaufort (Edgerly Plantation) County of Beaufort
State of S.C., before me, W. F. Schucking, a
Special Examiner of the Pension Office, personally appeared Ellie
Haines, who, being by me first duly sworn to answer
truly all interrogatories propounded to her during this Special Examination of aforesaid
pension claim, deposes and says: My age is about 70 years.
I don't know exactly. The white folks kept
the record of our ages, but I do not
know. No occupation, and postoffice
address as above.
I have lived on this plantation
all my life. Nearly all the people
on this Talbert plantation are there
yet. The Talberts were good to us all
and the slaves never left them. I
knew Susan Jones, the clt, and I
knew her husband, Jacob Jones
in his life time. I knew both
clt and her husband from their
childhood. I remember when clt. and
Jones were married. I was present
and saw them married. That was
long before the war. I can't give the
date, but it was ten or fifteen years
before the war. They were married at
Old House plantation, near here. They
were married by Deacon Dick Kelsin,
who was a deacon in the colored
Baptist Church on the place. Neither
the clt. or her husband Jacob
Jones had ever been married
before their marriage to each other.
